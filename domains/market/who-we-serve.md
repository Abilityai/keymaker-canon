# Who we serve

**Specialist recruiting firms, 20-100 people.** Agency recruiters placing permanent and contract
hires in one field (finance, tech, healthcare, engineering). Revenue is placement fees - roughly
20-25% of the candidate's first-year salary - so revenue per recruiter is the number the owner
watches.

## What they run
An ATS (Bullhorn, Loxo, Recruit CRM or similar), LinkedIn Recruiter, a shared inbox, a job board
or two, spreadsheets for client reporting.

## What hurts
- Recruiters spend most of the week on admin: sourcing lists, writing candidate summaries,
  formatting shortlists, chasing feedback, updating the ATS.
- Clients wait days for a shortlist and drift to a competitor.
- Good candidates go cold between first call and interview.
- Every recruiter does it their own way, so a leaver takes the method with them.

## What each offer means for them
| Offer | For a recruiting firm |
|---|---|
| Automation audit | Map what the desk already runs - ATS automations, sequences, prompts in someone's notes - and say what to keep, wrap or rebuild |
| Pilot | One desk live: a sourcing agent and a shortlist agent; the workflow is "brief in, client-ready shortlist out within 48 hours", a recruiter approves every send |
| Build | The firm's system: a desk per specialism, a shared brain of placements and candidate notes, one orchestrator, a workspace for every recruiter |
| Operate | We run it with them: monitoring, new playbooks as their markets change |

## How to qualify
1. A specialist recruiting firm, 20-100 people? Outside the field: answer, qualify, decline politely if there is no fit.
2. Who decides - the owner or the managing director?
3. How many open roles a recruiter carries, and how long a shortlist takes today.
4. Which ATS - the agents read and write it.

## How we talk
Lead with placements per recruiter and time to shortlist. Never promise a placement or a fee
figure for a named client. Candidate data is personal data: it stays on the client's own instance.
